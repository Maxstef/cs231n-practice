# Imitation learning, behavioral cloning, and DAgger

Imitation learning trains a policy from demonstrations of desired behavior. Instead of discovering actions only through trial, error, and reward, the learner receives examples of what an expert did in particular states.

## Behavioral cloning

Behavioral cloning treats imitation as supervised learning:

$$
\text{state or observation} \longrightarrow \text{expert action label}.
$$

A dataset contains pairs $(s_t,a_t^{expert})$, and the policy is trained to predict the expert action. This approach is simple and can use ordinary classification or regression losses.

For robots, demonstrations may come from teleoperation, human motion, scripted controllers, or an existing policy. Inputs might include images, language, and proprioception; outputs might be discrete commands, continuous joint controls, or short action sequences.

## Closed-loop covariate shift

Supervised action accuracy can be misleading because the training inputs were generated by the expert, while deployment inputs are generated by the learned policy:

$$
s_t \sim d_{expert}
\quad\text{during demonstration collection, but}\quad
s_t \sim d_{learner}
\quad\text{during deployment}.
$$

One small prediction error can move the learner away from the demonstrated trajectory. The new state may be unfamiliar, increasing the chance of another error and causing mistakes to compound. Evaluation must therefore run the policy in a closed loop rather than only score expert state–action pairs independently.

## Dataset aggregation

DAgger, short for Dataset Aggregation, reduces this distribution mismatch iteratively:

1. train on the current demonstration dataset;
2. execute the learned policy;
3. ask the expert for the correct actions in states visited by the learner;
4. aggregate those corrected pairs with the existing data and retrain.

The important addition is not merely more examples. DAgger obtains expert labels for **learner-induced states**, including recovery situations that a competent expert trajectory might rarely visit.

## Practical limitations

- Expert demonstrations and repeated expert queries can be expensive.
- Running an imperfect learner on a physical robot can be unsafe.
- Human experts may be inconsistent or suboptimal.
- The learner may copy visible behavior without recovering the expert's underlying intent.
- Performance still depends on observation quality and generalization beyond collected situations.
