# Augmenting human capabilities and healthcare

Automation is often framed as replacing human labor, but a human-centered objective is to **augment** people: help them notice, decide, coordinate, or perform tasks more safely and effectively.

## Ambient intelligence

Ambient intelligence combines sensors and machine learning with an environment such as a hospital or home. Instead of asking a person to operate a separate tool continuously, the system can summarize events that occur over time.

Potential healthcare uses include:

- detecting workflow events relevant to infection prevention;
- monitoring mobility, falls, sleep, or daily activities;
- identifying equipment or objects that may have been overlooked;
- reducing manual documentation and observer workload.

Continuous sensors can detect events that intermittent human observation misses, but this capability creates substantial privacy and governance obligations.

## Assistance is a system property

A model with good frame-level accuracy is not automatically useful. A deployed assistant must fit clinical workflow, communicate uncertainty, avoid alarm fatigue, protect patients and staff, and provide a path for human review.

Evaluation should therefore include:

- task-relevant sensitivity and false-alarm rates;
- performance across people, rooms, and institutions;
- effects on workload and outcomes;
- failure recovery and human override;
- privacy, consent, security, and data retention.

## Main intuition

The best division of labor may combine machine consistency with human context, judgment, empathy, and responsibility. The goal is not simply “AI versus humans,” but a better sociotechnical system made from both.
